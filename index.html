Learn ML Epistemology with Phoebe

Thornbury's triage model scores 0.949 on patients it never trained on. A year later the hospital group reorganises its wards and the same model, unchanged and never retrained, scores 0.765. Nothing in the pipeline was wrong: the split was honest, the metric was sensible, the number reproduced. What failed was an assumption nobody had written down. This is a course about the question underneath every accuracy figure, which is what licenses the belief that a model will work on data it has not seen. Six leader sessions on reading a claim, ten practitioner sessions on testing one, and two benches that train real networks in your browser so the arguments can be checked rather than repeated.

Leader track 🧾 · six sessions, no code

For whoever approves the deployment. What a score claims, why two equally good models are not the same model, what a benchmark licenses, how to treat evidence you did not produce, and the question to ask before your name goes on it.

What a test score entitles you to

A held-out number is a claim about a future that resembles the past. Three assumptions carry it, and one of them is nobody's fault when it breaks.

 🏆 Leader session 4

What a benchmark licenses

 🔄 Leader session 6

What would change your mind

The falsifier, written before the result. A claim with no falsifier is not a weak claim, it is not a claim.
